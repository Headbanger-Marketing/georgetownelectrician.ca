EV charger installation in Georgetown.

If the panel in your Georgetown home sits well away from the garage, a charger cable may need to cross finished rooms to reach it. The route, and what it means for walls and ceilings, belongs in the quote.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Georgetown property

Sketch the path from the panel to the parking spot in words, naming each finished room, ceiling or wall it might cross. Photograph the parking position and note whether the charger would have a timer or schedule.

Ask which route the contractor prefers and why, whether any ceiling or wall finish must be opened, and who will repair and repaint it afterwards. Ask for the repair to be named in the written scope.

What should the assessment establish?

A homeowner accepts or declines the proposed charger route with a written comparison in hand: the shortest path against any path that avoids finished rooms, and the reasons for the one proposed. In a Georgetown house with an attached garage, the quote sets out the mounting spot and how the circuit reaches it. The contractor explains the demand estimate and whether the charger fits within the supply alongside the other loads. Approved equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where the location needs it are named, with the party responsible for the ESA notification and whoever repairs the finishes.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Who patches the finishes once the charging cable is routed?

Only the written scope answers that, so do not assume it is included. The contractor can say whether patching, sealing and painting are part of the price or left to you or another trade.

Can the charger run on a schedule set by a timer?

Some chargers offer scheduling, but whether to rely on it is a design question. Ask the contractor whether a schedule changes the circuit or supply assessment, and keep the charger instructions for them to read.

Useful project details

  • Indoor or outdoor conditions

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
