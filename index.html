Before work begins

Check what the controls must work with first.

A townhouse may have no garage wiring, whereas a detached house with an attached garage can have separate switches for a hallway light and the garage ceiling, and a timer or sensor added to either could share a circuit with indoor rooms.

Note which switches you want to replace with dimmers, timers or smart devices, and photograph each existing switch plate and light fixture from the outside. Ask the contractor to confirm compatibility rather than assuming a control suits the wiring.

Can any dimmer be fitted to the lights I already have?

Not always. Whether a dimmer suits a fixture depends on the bulb type, the fixture and the existing wiring. The contractor should check the product information for each light before anything is bought or installed.

Should garage lights and outlets be planned with the house?

Garage lighting and receptacles may be wired together with indoor rooms, so a change in one place can affect the other. Ask the contractor to trace which spaces each circuit feeds, then record the result in the written scope.

How do I compare quotes for smart switches and timers?

Comparison needs every contractor to list the same items: which rooms and devices are included, what existing wiring is retained and what the written scope says about compatibility. Quotes that leave these out are hard to compare.

Who has to approve new controls or added wiring in a home?

That depends on the work. Replacing a control and adding new wiring are different jobs, and almost all electrical work needs an ESA notification that the contractor files. A building permit is a separate matter, so ask the municipality.

If I submit my details here, who confirms the dimmer work?

A Licensed Electrical Contractor confirms the work separately: who it is and its licence, which you should check through ESA before agreeing, as well as the scope and cost and the dates.
